How much does the inner aspect change the meaning? Often very little: both resulting verbs are perfective, and both can describe the same completed set of arrivals, departures, or transfers.

From an inner perfective: a useful compositional reading is “a number of individual completed arrivals / departures / transfers”. From an inner imperfective: “arriving / leaving / transferring distributed over a number of participants”. Outer по- still bounds the whole set, so this is not an ongoing or habitual imperfective.

For example, они́ поуе́хали and они́ поуезжа́ли can both mean “they all went away”. The latter can suggest people leaving successively or dispersing over time, but this is a contextual tendency, not a fixed contrast: the former can describe successive departures too.

Do not read the columns as “all completed” versus “some still incomplete”, or “once” versus “habitually”. The larger difference for many families is which formation sounds familiar or natural; a productive form may be much less established than its neighbour.

How to read this grid & references

Both ла́зить and ла́зать are multidirectional climbing verbs. Their first-person singular forms are ла́жу and ла́заю, respectively; both are shown in the same cell.

The pronoun selector chooses the example subject for finite forms and is hidden for infinitives. Past agrees in number and gender, not person, so it can use я or ты too. Present and future allow all three persons; their gender comes from the pronoun and does not change the verb ending, so the gender controls appear only for singular past forms. Number is shared across tenses; gender is shared across past singular and third-person singular. Choosing я or ты preserves a stored masculine/feminine choice, which can be changed independently for their past forms. Neuter singular uses оно́. Hidden selections are retained. A perfective verb has no present tense, so its present cell shows a dash. Future is simple for perfective verbs and буду / бу́дем + infinitive for imperfective verbs. With по-, the right column means doing the activity for a while; it is not the imperfective partner of the left column.

The по- checkbox without a directional prefix retains the two single-prefix meanings. With при- or у-, outer по- is distributive: all or many movers arrive or leave successively. The operation can use either inner aspect stem; the result is perfective in both columns. Intransitive verbs distribute over movers; transitive verbs can distribute over moved objects or people.

Singular intransitive distributive forms are struck through for a single-individual reading. They are not morphologically ungrammatical: a collective singular subject can supply multiple participants. Transitive forms remain valid with a singular subject when multiple objects supply the distribution. “Productive / uncommon” distinguishes compositional formations from familiar lexical examples; it is not an error label.

The prefixed columns follow the corresponding base families, including conventional stem changes. A cell is judged in its movement sense; other dictionary meanings do not make it ungrammatical.

For an explicit example of the two derivations overlapping in meaning, see Zinova, Russian Verbal Prefixation, pp. 140–142 (PDF pp. 154–156): both perfective-derived and imperfective-derived forms are used for the same group running out. The conceptual paraphrases above are a reading aid, not a claim that every pair has a stable semantic opposition.
